# Commerce Visuals

Start by locking one Campaign Style Lock for the complete set: no more than three colors, one lighting system, one background family, a product-coverage target, and explicit no-drift rules. The hero must show one complete sellable product in one verified colorway on a clean near-white background, centered with useful margins. Exclude unrelated props, collages, badges, prices, promotional text, watermarks, platform marks, and invented logos. A clean adult apparel model may be used only when the garment remains the dominant, unobstructed subject; children’s products remain product-only.

Treat the five detail slots as a conversion sequence, and give every slot one primary claim: overall silhouette, neckline/closure evidence, sleeve/cuff/material detail, back/hem construction or real source-supported variants, and practical fit/use or a verified seller size chart. Translate visible features into conservative buyer value without inventing performance claims. Do not use multiple crops or poses of the same view as nominally different detail images. When only one verified color exists, the variant slot must become a back/construction slot rather than fabricating a lineup.

Reference pixels define silhouette, neckline or waistband, sleeve or leg length, closures, pockets, trims, pattern, color, and product marks. Reject identity drift, invented construction, malformed anatomy, unreadable generated text, unrelated products, severe blur, and obvious compositing artifacts.

Critical product structure must remain unambiguous in every accepted slot. A crop, fold, pose, flat lay, or variant grid must not hide a category-defining feature so thoroughly that a long-sleeve item reads as sleeveless, a full-length item reads as cropped, or the verified neckline/closure cannot be recognized. In a variant lineup, each displayed item must visibly remain the same product rather than merely sharing its color or fabric.

Do not confuse a contaminated but readable source with an unusable reference. A person, lifestyle background, ordinary prop, or removable background brand can remain an edit reference when the product is clear; the generated candidate must remove all such contamination and pass the strict hero/detail gate before delivery. Hard content risks such as QR codes, contact information, watermarks, price/review graphics, platform marks, or sensitive content remain blocked.

Evaluate the set-level A6 threshold explicitly: at least five of the six images must be independently usable. Perceptual-hash difference is necessary but not sufficient: each detail must also satisfy its assigned commercial job. Near-duplicate crops of one scene do not count as distinct usable slots. A source fallback that fails the intended slot, contains unrelated branding or props, or violates the hero composition is not rescued merely by correct dimensions.

When feedback identifies a weak artifact, request a targeted regeneration with the defect and desired correction stated explicitly. Preserve the current artifact until the revised candidate passes selection. Do not solve an aesthetic defect by automatically replacing the artifact with a lower-value source fallback.

Use culturally neutral, contemporary marketplace styling for the US, South Korea, and Brazil. Avoid flags, landmarks, clichés, body reshaping, or tokenized representation.
